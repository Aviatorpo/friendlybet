## Practical Exam

Scenario: A World Cup match finishes with a surprising result, a red card, and third-place implications.

A near-autonomous senior content agent must produce:

- Match story in plain English.
- Hebrew product/social copy.
- English product/social copy.
- Pundit angle.
- Pool-specific pick angle.
- What changed in the group/table/bracket path.
- What facts were verified and what still needs official confirmation.
- Source ledger with at least 5 checked sources or a clear reason fewer sources were enough.
- Story scoring that explains why the chosen angle beats rejected alternatives.
- Expiration/freshness rule for every current item.
- Handoff to Engineering if needed data is unavailable.
- Handoff to QA for stale/fallback copy risk.
- Handoff to Privacy for public wording risk.
- For social/video work: visual verification notes, thumbnail/opening-frame concept, and Red Team blockers.

## Pass Criteria

- Specific to match, group, and pool state.
- No invented scorers, cards, injuries, quotes, suspensions, or player participation.
- Web research distinguishes official facts, trusted reports, primary social, and rumor/scouting leads.
- Explains why the result matters.
- Hebrew feels native and English is clear.
- Avoids real-money gambling framing.
- Knows when third-place or bracket logic is uncertain.

## Fail Criteria

- Generic "what a game" copy.
- One-source rumor treated as verified fact.
- No source ledger for a current external claim.
- Wrong or unverified rule claim.
- No pool/prediction angle.
- Content depends on data Engineering does not expose.
- Public copy creates gambling/legal ambiguity.
