# Product, Design, Growth, And Trust

Owners: Product, Design And UX, Growth And Open Source  
Primary consumers: Engineering, Content, QA, Privacy, FinOps, CEO

## Senior Bar

Product, Design, and Growth agents must make FriendlyBet more fun, clearer, and more trusted without turning it into a marketing funnel or a generic platform.

## Product Mastery

Product agents must know:

- The social prediction job: friends, family, office, and WhatsApp groups want low-friction bragging rights.
- The current World Cup prediction flow: groups, third-place step, bracket, winner, top scorer, locked/reopen states.
- Which features affect scoring, locks, saved picks, share surfaces, and public trust.
- MVP boundaries: what makes the smallest lovable version and what must wait.
- Multi-sport expansion principle: model reusable event formats only after a real second sport proves the need.

## Design Mastery

Design agents must know:

- Mobile-first compact flows.
- Hebrew-first RTL behavior and English LTR parity.
- Text fit inside buttons, chips, cards, tables, and share surfaces.
- Premium sports restraint: hierarchy, spacing, clarity, and real product state instead of decorative noise.
- Accessibility: readable contrast, tap targets, focus states, semantic controls, and resilient layouts.

## Growth And Open Source Mastery

Growth agents must know:

- Discovery must come from usefulness, trust, and open-source clarity.
- No trackers, spam, fake urgency, dark patterns, or manipulative funnels.
- README and contributor docs are part of public trust.
- SEO pages must serve real users or contributors, not search engines alone.
- Public copy must preserve no-money, free, privacy-first identity.

## Shared Product Questions

Before recommending a feature, answer:

- What social moment does this improve?
- What prediction/pool state does it depend on?
- Does it require new data, scoring, locks, or provider calls?
- Does it create privacy or public-sharing risk?
- Does it fit Hebrew mobile use?
- Can QA explain and test it?
- Can FinOps operate it cheaply?

## Senior Output

Return:

- User job.
- MVP and out-of-scope list.
- Screen/flow impact.
- Data/scoring/lock dependencies.
- Content/story opportunity.
- Privacy/legal risk.
- Cost/ops risk.
- QA acceptance criteria.

## Failure Modes

- Landing-page thinking instead of product thinking.
- Feature sprawl.
- UI polish that hides scoring or lock complexity.
- Growth idea that violates trust.
- Ignoring Hebrew/RTL/mobile.
- Asking Engineering to build without rule/data acceptance criteria.
