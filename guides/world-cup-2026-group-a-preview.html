World Cup 2026 Group A Preview: Mexico, South Korea, South Africa & Czech Republic

A complete preview of Group A — where a co-host nation meets Asian consistency, African pride and European quality. Here's how to call it right in your prediction pool.

Group A of the 2026 World Cup carries one unmissable storyline: Mexico, one of three co-host nations, opens the tournament with home support behind them. Hosting transforms a team's World Cup experience — the crowd, the travel advantage, the national expectation — and for prediction purposes, it makes Mexico a near-certain pick to top the group. But the race below them is genuinely open, and that is where Group A can make or break a prediction pool.

South Korea bring Asia's most reliable World Cup pedigree; Czech Republic arrive with deep European tournament experience; and South Africa carry both the legacy of 2010 and the hunger to prove they belong at the highest level. Four teams, three games each, and a leaderboard waiting to be written.

Mexico: the home-soil favourite

Mexico automatically qualified for the 2026 World Cup as one of the three North American co-hosts alongside the United States and Canada. That automatic berth means El Tri's preparation has been entirely focused on performance rather than qualification, and the squad has had full cycles to build cohesion under home conditions.

The home-crowd factor in football is hard to overstate. Mexico's fan culture is among the most intense in the world, and playing at their own stadiums — venues they know intimately — gives them a structural advantage that statistics struggle to capture. Mexico have reached the knockout rounds of every World Cup since 1978, and in a group stage that rewards consistent performances over three games, their combination of home advantage and tournament experience makes them the clear pick to finish first.

For anyone filling in a prediction pool, "Mexico top" is the safe, consensus call. It is also almost certainly correct. The question is what you do with the three positions below them.

South Korea: Asia's most reliable World Cup team

South Korea's story at the 2002 World Cup — reaching the semi-finals on home soil and eliminating heavyweight European sides along the way — established them as a genuine force in international football. Since then, they have been the most consistent Asian qualifier for the World Cup, combining collective discipline with a strong European-based professional generation that understands the demands of the highest level.

The Korean system is built on relentless pressing, organised defensive shape and an ability to hurt opponents on the counter. Against Mexico they will be heavy underdogs, but against Czech Republic and South Africa, South Korea can legitimately be considered favourites. In a tight group where results among the three lower-ranked sides often decide who advances, South Korea's tactical reliability makes them the natural pick for second place.

If you want to differ from the consensus prediction, the contrarian call is Czech Republic leapfrogging South Korea — and it is not a crazy one. But the safe second-place pick is South Korea.

Czech Republic: Europe's quiet qualifier

Czech Republic arrive at 2026 carrying a football identity shaped over decades of Central European precision — organised, hard to break down, and tactically flexible. Their history includes Euro 1996 finalists as the Czech Republic (and Euro 1976 champions as Czechoslovakia), and the modern team has maintained that ethos of collective quality over individual flair.

In a group that pits them primarily against South Korea and South Africa for qualifying positions, Czech Republic's European quality is a genuine differentiator. They are the kind of side that can hold a 1-0 lead for ninety minutes and frustrate more technically gifted opponents into mistakes. A Czech Republic side that starts well is dangerous — and a Czech Republic side that tops their head-to-head results against South Korea could be the story of Group A.

For prediction pools, picking Czech Republic to finish third (ahead of South Africa, behind South Korea) is the moderate call. Picking them second is the bold one.

South Africa: the 2010 legacy nation

There is a particular weight that comes with being the country that brought the World Cup to Africa for the first time. South Africa's 2010 tournament — the vuvuzelas, the Jabulani ball, the electric atmosphere — introduced a continent to a new scale of football celebration. Bafana Bafana now return to a World Cup stage carrying both that legacy and a point to prove.

On paper, South Africa are the group's outsiders. But the expanded 48-team format of 2026 — with eight of the best third-placed teams advancing to the Round of 32 — means even a third-place finish might not be the end of the road. In a group where Mexico figures to take first easily, South Africa's best chance of any drama is stealing results against South Korea or Czech Republic. One good evening can reshape a group.

In prediction pools, calling a South Africa upset is the high-risk, high-reward move. It probably won't pay off — but when it does, it can be the pick that wins you the whole table.

Group A FAQ

Which teams are in World Cup 2026 Group A?
Mexico, South Africa, South Korea and Czech Republic.
Who is favourite to win Group A?
Mexico are the clear favourites to top the group as one of the three co-host nations.
How many teams advance from each group at WC 2026?
The top two from every group qualify automatically, plus the eight best third-placed sides across all 12 groups — meaning a total of 32 teams advance.
Why does Mexico have a home advantage at WC 2026?
Mexico is one of three co-host nations alongside the United States and Canada. Group stage games for Mexico will be played in their own domestic stadiums in front of their home fans.
