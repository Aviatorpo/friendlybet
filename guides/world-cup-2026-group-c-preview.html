On paper, Group C looks like a gentle stroll for Brazil. Look a little closer and it's one of the spicier groups in the whole draw: a Morocco side that already gatecrashed a World Cup semi-final, a Scotland team that lives to ruin a favourite's afternoon, and a Haiti outfit that genuinely doesn't care what the bookmakers think. For your prediction pool, the points aren't in saying "Brazil win the group" — everyone says that. The points are in calling the chaos underneath. Let's break it down team by team.

🔍 The four teams, rated

Brazil

★ Group favourite

Five stars, five World Cups, and an attacking pool deep enough to field two dangerous front lines. Brazil's ceiling is "win the whole thing," and a group like this is exactly where the Seleção are meant to flex. The only real question is whether they cruise through on autopilot or get caught napping in a tricky middle game — they have history of both.

🏆 Who tops the group?

Brazil. Next question. Across three group games, elite depth usually wins out, and the Seleção have more match-winners than the rest of the group combined. Putting Brazil first is the safe, sensible call — just remember it scores the same for you as it does for everyone else in your pool, so it won't move you up the leaderboard on its own.

⚔️ The real battle: second place

This is where Group C is actually decided, and where your pool points live. Morocco are the smart-money pick: defensively miserly, lethal on the break, and battle-hardened from a deep World Cup run. But Scotland are exactly the kind of disciplined, physical side that grinds out a 1–0 and parks the points in the bank. Back Morocco and you're with the crowd; back Scotland to sneak second and — if it lands — you've just found yourself some serious daylight on the leaderboard.

🔥 Hot take: Don't sleep on a Morocco–Scotland draw deciding everything on goal difference. If you fancy a tiebreaker thriller, get your group-stage scorelines bold — the safe 1–0s won't separate you from your mates.

🌶️ Could Haiti cook up a shock?

History says outsiders nick one big result per World Cup, and the new 48-team format is practically designed to keep underdogs alive — eight of the twelve third-placed teams advance, so a single famous night can carry a "weaker" side into the knockouts. Predicting a Haiti upset is high-risk, high-reward: get it wrong and you've burned a pick, get it right and you might just win the whole pool with one gutsy call.
