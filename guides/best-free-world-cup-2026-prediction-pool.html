Why "free" usually has an asterisk

Commercial pool apps have to make money somewhere. That means a paid tier, advertising, or monetising your attention and data — which is fine as a business model, but it is the opposite of what most people want for a casual pool with friends or colleagues. FriendlyBet is a non-commercial, open-source hobby project, so there is no asterisk: no paid tier, no ads, and nothing to upsell.

The privacy difference, concretely: FriendlyBet asks for a nickname and nothing else. Account recovery uses a 16-character code that is SHA-256 hashed in your browser before it ever reaches the database, so only a hash is stored. There is no email list, no profile, and no sensitive data that could leak — which is exactly why it suits a privacy-conscious workplace.

What you actually get to predict

Feature-wise it is not a stripped-down free tier. You rank all 12 group standings, build the full official Round-of-32 knockout bracket through to the Final (including assigning the eight best third-placed teams), and call the Golden Boot top scorer. Your knockout bracket is generated from your own group predictions, so it is a real test of your forecast. Scoring uses a doubling progression that rewards harder, later rounds, and runs as a deterministic, automatic job — no manual tallying and no arguments.
